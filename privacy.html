Privacy Policy

Effective 21 October 2026

The short version

  • Clasp has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Your diary is kept on your iPhone and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage. I can't see it.
  • The text of your pages is encrypted on your iPhone before it goes to iCloud.
  • I don't collect any personal data through the app.

Who I am

Clasp is made by Aarihant Malliah, an independent developer ("I", "me"). I'm responsible for the personal data I hold, which is only what you send me by email (see If you email me); for that, I'm the "data controller", in legal terms. Your diary is held by you and Apple, not me. I'm also the person to contact with questions or complaints about privacy. Email asm.business03@gmail.com.

This policy covers the Clasp app and this website. Using Clasp is also covered by the Terms of Use.

What Clasp stores, and where

On your iPhone

  • Your diary pages: what you write, the date of each page, any folded corners and creases, and when each page was created and last edited.
  • Your diary's look: the year, cover and paper choice, whether the leather ages, a random number that decides where the wear appears, and running counts of days written, times opened and words written. These counts are used only to age the cover.
  • Preferences: ink style, page sounds, haptics, the lock and when it relocks, whether the app icon matches the cover, whether pages print the date, and similar choices. These stay on that iPhone.

Clasp keeps this in its own storage on your iPhone, which other apps can't read. Like any app's data, it is included in backups of your iPhone (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) if you make them.

In your iCloud

If you're signed in to iCloud and iCloud is turned on for Clasp, your diary pages and your diary's look (above) are also saved to your own private iCloud database, so they sync between your devices and can come back on a new iPhone, once they've uploaded (that needs an internet connection and free iCloud storage). Preferences are not synced.

  • This storage is provided by Apple and is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy and the iCloud terms.
  • Only your Apple Account can access it. I can't see, read or change your entries.
  • Apple encrypts everything Clasp keeps in iCloud while it travels and while it's stored on Apple's servers.

How your writing is encrypted in iCloud

  • The text of your pages and your folded corners are encrypted on your iPhone before they're uploaded, and only decrypted after they come back down to one of your devices.
  • With Advanced Data Protection for iCloud turned on (Settings app > your name > iCloud), only your devices hold the keys for that text, so it's end-to-end encrypted and Apple can't read it either.
  • With standard data protection (the iCloud default), it's still encrypted, but Apple keeps the keys so it can help you recover your data.
  • Not covered by that extra encryption: the date of each page, when it was created and last edited, and your diary's look and counts. Apple still encrypts these in transit and on its servers, but holds the keys, even with Advanced Data Protection on. So Apple's systems can tell which days have a page, but not what's written on it when Advanced Data Protection is on.

Apple explains these protections in iCloud data security overview.

Turning iCloud off

You can turn iCloud off for Clasp in the Settings app (tap your name, then iCloud), or sign out of iCloud. iOS then removes the synced pages from that iPhone; they stay in your iCloud and should come back if you turn iCloud on again. Pages written in the moments before, while offline, may not have reached iCloud yet and can be lost, so connect to the internet and open Clasp for a minute first.

The lock (Touch ID, Face ID or passcode)

You can choose to lock your diary. When you unlock it, iOS checks your fingerprint, face or passcode and only tells Clasp whether the check succeeded. Your fingerprint, face data and passcode never reach Clasp or me. While the lock is on, Clasp also hides your pages in the app switcher.

The lock stops the diary opening in Clasp. It doesn't add encryption of its own, and it doesn't change what's stored in iCloud, which follows the section above.

Exporting a PDF

When you export a week or a month, Clasp makes the PDF on your iPhone and opens the iOS share sheet. You decide where it goes (Files, Mail, Print, another app). Once you share it, the copy is handled by wherever you sent it. Clasp deletes its own temporary copy when you close the export screen, or the next time Clasp starts if it was closed mid-export.

What Clasp does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in of its own.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking or third-party code libraries (SDKs).
  • No servers of mine. The only thing Clasp sends over the internet is your diary syncing with your own iCloud, through Apple. Clasp shows this policy, its Terms of Use and its Support page inside the app, from copies built into it, and doesn't open web pages.
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone. Clasp doesn't send you notifications.
  • I don't sell or share personal data, because I don't have any from the app.

Who else is involved

I don't share your data with anyone. Three companies provide services that Clasp, this website and my email rely on, each under its own privacy policy:

  • Apple stores your diary in iCloud (above), runs the App Store and handles your purchase. I don't receive your name or payment details. If you've chosen in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give me crash reports and anonymous, combined usage statistics; you can turn this off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements. iOS features you use while writing, such as the keyboard, dictation or Writing Tools, are provided by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub hosts this website (GitHub Pages). GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security, under the GitHub Privacy Statement. This site uses no cookies, scripts or analytics, and everything on its pages, including the fonts and images, is served from the site itself, so reading it doesn't contact any other website.
  • Google provides my email (Gmail). If you email me, your message is stored by Google under the Google Privacy Policy.

If you email me

If you contact support, I receive your email address and whatever you write. I use it only to reply to you and to fix the problem you tell me about, and I delete support emails within 12 months of our last message (Gmail may keep deleted mail in Trash for up to 30 more days). Please don't send diary entries unless you want me to see them. You never have to email me to use Clasp.

Why I'm allowed to use it

  • UK and EU (GDPR): my legitimate interest in answering questions about Clasp and fixing problems, or, when you need help with Clasp after buying it, to give you the support that comes with it.
  • Elsewhere: you send it to me voluntarily so that I can answer you, and I use it only for that. If you tell me to stop, I'll delete it.

Where it's handled

Google may store email on servers in other countries, including the United States, and I may not be in the country you write from. So your email may be handled outside your country, where data protection laws may differ from yours. I keep it only as long as stated above and use it only to reply to you.

Children

Clasp collects no personal data from anyone, including children. If a child has emailed me and a parent or guardian would like that email deleted, write to me and I'll delete it.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your diary stays until you delete it. You're in control:

  • A page, in Clasp: select its text and delete it, and drag a folded corner back to unfold it. The change syncs to your other devices and iCloud. Clasp keeps the empty page for that date, and the counts that age the cover never go down.
  • This iPhone: deleting the Clasp app removes its data from that iPhone. If you use iCloud, your diary stays in iCloud and comes back if you reinstall. Older iPhone backups keep their copy until they're replaced or deleted.
  • Everything in iCloud: open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud > Storage (or Manage Account Storage). If Clasp is listed, tap it, then Delete Data from iCloud. This removes your diary from iCloud and from all your devices and can't be undone, so export anything you want to keep first. Menu names can vary between iOS versions; if you can't find Clasp there, email me and I'll help you find the right setting.

Because I have no access to your diary, I can't delete it for you, and I can't recover it if it's lost.

Your choices and rights

You can stop Clasp using iCloud at any time by turning iCloud off for it (see "Turning iCloud off" above).

Depending on where you live, laws such as the EU and UK GDPR, or similar data protection laws, may give you rights to access, correct or delete your personal data, to object to or restrict how it's used, to get a copy to take elsewhere, to withdraw consent, or to name someone to use your rights if you can't. Your diary is held only by you and Apple, so you use those rights directly in Clasp, on your devices and in iCloud, as described above. For anything I do hold (such as a support email), write to asm.business03@gmail.com and I'll answer within 30 days.

If you're unhappy with how I've handled your data, please tell me first so I can put it right. You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, the authority in the country where you live; elsewhere, the authority where you live, if there is one.

California residents: I don't sell or share personal information, as California law defines those words, and I don't use it for targeted advertising.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top. If Clasp ever starts collecting personal data, I'll say so here and in the App Store privacy details before that version is released, and ask for your consent where the law requires it.
